## 🎯 Decision Question
Which project management system would best support a small remote executive team that needs clear task ownership, executive visibility, collaboration, documentation, and workflow automation?

## 📊 Executive Bottom Line
Based on the evidence, the recommended approach is **Asana**.

## 🔍 Key Findings
- Asana provides the strongest executive-oriented project visibility of the three, with dashboards, portfolio reporting, custom fields, status updates, and multiple project views.
- Asana is well suited to cross-functional teams because its workflow covers task ownership, deadlines, dependencies, reporting, forms, automation, and integrations.
- Notion is the strongest option when documentation and project information need to live together. Its databases, pages, timelines, dependencies, and customizable views provide significant flexibility.
- Notion requires more workspace design and configuration because teams can build their own workflows rather than adopting a more structured project-management system.
- Linear is highly capable for product and engineering teams, particularly where issue tracking, cycles, roadmaps, and development workflows are central.
- Linear's Slack integration is strong, but its product positioning is primarily around product development rather than general executive administration.
- For a small remote executive team, executive reporting and cross-functional coordination are more important than specialized software-development workflows.

## 📋 Options Analysis
### Asana
**Pros:** Strong executive reporting and dashboards, Clear task ownership and deadlines, Multiple project views including timeline and Gantt, Workflow automation and custom fields, Strong cross-functional collaboration, Integrations with Slack and productivity tools
**Cons:** Some advanced reporting capabilities require higher-tier plans, Less flexible than Notion for building a fully customized knowledge system, Can introduce more structure than a very small team may initially need

### Notion
**Pros:** Excellent documentation and knowledge management, Highly customizable databases and workflows, Projects, tasks, documentation, and meeting information can coexist, Supports timelines, dependencies, multiple views, and automation, Strong flexibility for evolving team processes
**Cons:** Requires more initial workspace design, Highly flexible systems can become inconsistent without governance, Executive reporting is less purpose-built than Asana's dashboard approach

### Linear
**Pros:** Excellent issue and project tracking, Strong product-development workflows, Fast and structured workflow, Strong Slack integration, Useful insights and planning capabilities
**Cons:** Primarily designed around product and software development, Less suitable for general executive administration, May introduce unnecessary technical structure for a non-engineering executive team
